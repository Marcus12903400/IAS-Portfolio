Alongside it: 190 macOS ._* junk files, three dead hardcoded paths from the original author's Mac (/Users/joshuamorell/…), and five competing launchers across four folders. ASK 1

Use more of the computer

"I checked task manager while it was running and i had no cpu usage… lets shoot for 50%"

Confirmed and measured: the pipeline sat at 4.2% of 24 cores — exactly 1.00 core. There was no parallelism anywhere in the codebase.

The instinct is to add threads. Measurement said otherwise. libigl — which dominates the slowest stage — holds the GIL (parallel efficiency 0.15 on 6 threads), so threads do nothing for it. The real lever turned out to be vectorisation.

The single biggest win: estimate_principal_curvatures ran a separate least-squares solve for every one of 2 million faces in a Python loop. A triangle has at most three neighbours, so each solve is tiny and fixed-size — they all now accumulate into one batched solve. 14.6 s → 0.65 s on a test mesh, agreeing with the old path to 1.6×10-9 relative.

Panels also develop in parallel worker processes now — but only when it pays. On your Key West scan the surface raster is 276 MB and Windows copies it to every worker; measured, five workers made that stage slower, because one panel dominates the work anyway. It falls back to in-process above a configurable budget and says so in the log. On the smaller test boat, where the raster is small and two panels are comparable, the fan-out gives 1.7× on that stage.

Set it under parallel in engine/config/default.yaml, or per run with AUTODECK_WORKERS. The worker count provably does not change the cache key or the geometry: a parallel run and a serial run produced byte-identical DXF output — 166 entities, maximum coordinate difference exactly 0 mm.

ASK 2

Texture

"open the whole obj folder with the texture files as well… use the texture to help with outline drawing detection"

Opening a folder, and seeing it

Open a scan folder… takes the whole export — .obj, .mtl and the images — and the deck renders with its real photographic surface under a new Texture (scan photo) shading mode, on by default when a scan has one. Atlases are downscaled to 4096 px before being sent to the browser; a 16384² atlas is 268 megapixels, which trips Pillow's decompression guard and exceeds the texture limit on many integrated GPUs.

Using it to find the deck edge

The colour change between deck and gelcoat is added to the boundary evidence, with two deliberate constraints that keep it from making a good geometric result worse.

Chromaticity, not brightness. Shadows and exposure drift multiply a pixel by a scalar, and chromaticity is invariant to that — so a baked shadow across the deck is nearly invisible to this measure while teak against gelcoat is not.

Region colour, not neighbour colour. This one was found by testing: a plain edge-to-edge colour gradient fired on 11% of flat deck interior — those are the teak caulk lines, 63.5 mm apart and near-black against the plank. It would have added cost right across the deck and fragmented it. Averaging over a 90 mm region first erases the pattern and keeps the material step: false fires 11.0% → 0.3%, and separation between the wall line and flat deck went from 2× to 56×.

Colour is additive only — excluded from the decisive-evidence test — so it can reinforce a boundary the geometry already suspects but can never declare one alone. A stain cannot invent a wall line. On the test boat the resulting outline came out byte-identical to the geometry-only run.

ASK 3

Seams and sheet nesting

Sheet
40 × 80 in — 1016 × 2032 mm
Largest part
39 × 79 in — 990.6 × 2006.6 mm (12.7 mm margin all round)
Seam gap
6.0 mm between pieces that meet at a seam
Piece gap
20.0 mm minimum on the sheet, adjustable in the page
Rotation
0° or 180° only — never 90°, never mirrored

Draw a seam by dragging a line on the Flat layout & seams tab; drag either end to move it, and the sheets re-plan on every edit. The Sheet layout tab shows the nested result, and Export sheet DXFs writes one sheet_NN.dxf per sheet.

Because TruGrain is directional, the whole job is rotated so the boat's long axis runs along the 2032 mm sheet dimension, after which nesting is ordinary bottom-left packing. 180° is free — it keeps the grain and lets an L-shaped piece tuck into its neighbour's notch, which is where the material saving comes from.

The seam gap is produced by subtracting a 3 mm kerf either side of the line, which gives the asymmetry the job needs for nothing: the fitted outer boundary is untouched and only the new seam edges are inset. Arcs survive the cut — 22 of 23 preserved on the real fitted panel, worst rebuild error 0.0094 mm — rather than degrading to dense polylines.

Check before cutting

The boat axis is detected with a confidence, and on the test run that confidence was 0.21. When it is low the page says so and you can type the grain angle in by hand. If you do, and it disagrees with the pattern already baked into the fitted DXF by more than 3°, you get a second warning — the planks and the grain would not line up.

FOUND

Bugs in the existing cut geometry

These were all in code that reported success. They are the findings I would most want you to see.

Silent · scale

A failed geodesic quietly rescaled the panel

When igl.exact_geodesic raised, the code silently fell back to a shortest path along mesh edges. That distance is the developed panel's scale, and ARAP does not absorb it — I measured the fallback at +7.36%, or +394 mm on a 5.4 m panel, with no warning anywhere. The panel is now rejected instead of mis-scaled.

Silent · material

Seam splitting lost a quarter of a square metre

Arc provenance was attached to ring vertices. A straight kerf cut arrives with only two vertices and both sit on the original boundary, so the seam edge was never recognised and the piece closed with a diagonal across the cut. Kerf removed went from 254,868 mm² to 21,399 mm² once fixed, piece area from 2.3780 m² back to 2.6115 m², and preserved arcs from 16 to 22. An independent review measured the same defect as 10.5% of the material missing from the DXF while the report said OK.

Silent · wrong panel

A seam cut panels you never drew across

Seams are extended before cutting so a roughly drawn line severs the panel cleanly — but the extension reached a full bounding-box diagonal in both directions, so a seam drawn over one panel sliced another panel metres away. Global seams now apply only where the line you actually drew crosses.

Misleading picture

The sheet preview drew the wrong frame

The preview resolved the boat axis separately from the exporter and ignored the manual grain override, so the pieces were placed in one frame and drawn in another. With a 45° override every piece fell outside the sheet — and that bites exactly when detection is unsure and you have been told to override.

Wasted material

Spacing reserved far more than you asked

The clearance buffer used mitre joins, which at a sharp corner run well past the nominal offset — 80.6 mm beyond a round buffer at a 20 mm setting. Pieces were pushed apart and could spill onto an extra sheet.

Also fixed

Two smaller ones

Exported piece outlines were wound clockwise while the rest of the codebase enforces counter-clockwise outer rings; and the sheet DXFs could never appear in the Files panel, because the preview path never reported the files it had written.

LIMITS

What is not proven

Stated plainly, because none of this has cut material yet.

  • Texture detection is unvalidated on a real scan. Your Key West OBJ has no .mtl and no texture, so every threshold is calibrated on the synthetic boat I generated. It degrades to geometry-only when there is no texture, which is what happens on your real scan today.
  • The nester is a first-fit heuristic, not an optimiser. It respects every constraint and is deterministic, but it will not always find the tightest packing.
  • Development and mapping are still the slow stages, at 153 s and 56 s of the 405 s. An independent survey found the mapping cost is one AABB tree rebuilt per curve — 84 rebuilds of the same tree — which should batch down to a couple of seconds. I did not do it; it is the obvious next win.
  • Manufacturing approval remains TEST_ONLY until a human has physically verified a cut. Nothing here changes that.
