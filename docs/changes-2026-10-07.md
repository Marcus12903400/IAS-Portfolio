# AutoDeck 5.4 — changes from 5.3

Written 2026-10-07. This is the owner-facing record of everything that changed
when the 2026-10-07 seam/sheet audit's fix plan (steps 1–9 of
`docs/seam-audit-2026-10-07.md` section 6) was implemented. Each entry says
what you will see differently, and why. The audit's own finding ids (A1, B1,
E2 …) are quoted so the two documents can be read side by side.

The engine suite went from 297 to 327 passing tests (one new file,
`engine/tests/test_audit_fixes.py`, plus tightened pins in the existing ones).
The frozen nesting table was re-captured once, by the book (see "The frozen
nesting table" below).

## Owner decisions that were made for you

The audit left fifteen questions open (its section 7). Nine blocked coding, and
the audit lead's recommendation was taken for each, with one deliberate
exception:

| Question | Audit recommendation | What 5.4 does |
|---|---|---|
| A1 chord or whole line? | chord-only for seams with a direction; legacy free seams keep the whole line | **Taken.** A seam placed with the direction tool (or by the optimiser) cuts exactly the chord you clicked, plus one seam-gap of reach past the outline. Free two-point seams from old runs cut the whole line exactly as before. |
| A2 narrowest piece worth cutting | min width 40–50 mm, settable | **Adjusted to 15 mm.** The audit's own "not bugs" list protects the 25–40 mm gunwale-strip pieces, which a 40 mm floor would silently delete. 15 mm removes every sliver the audit measured (5–10 mm wide) and keeps every real part. Settable as `min_piece_width_mm` in the sheets settings. |
| A4 may a seam sit on a hatch/console edge? | only with the kerf offset out of the material | **Taken, in the safe direction:** a seam that settles onto a fitted edge now stops half a gap short of it, on the far side, so the kerf never crosses into the deck — the join still reads as running on the edge, the piece keeps its fitted outline, and a hatch is never widened. |
| C2 cut-out clearance | 30–40 mm | **30 mm**, with the refinement that a cut is only refused when it actually reaches past a cut-out's rim (8 mm in) or grazes a round one — a cut running ON a wall is the tidy cut and stays. |
| C5 minimum arrangements before writing | at least 50 | **Taken**, refined: the answer is only withheld when the CLOCK ran out under 50 arrangements; a search that ran to completion has had its look however few it took. |
| E2 block or queue edits during the search | block while the job runs | **Taken.** Seam edits answer "a job is already running" while the optimiser or an export is running, and the page holds them. |
| E6 seam gap floor | 2 mm | **Taken.** The box and the server both refuse under 2 mm, so the state where "remove" stopped working cannot be reached from the page. |
| E10 keep the first hand-placed set under a stable name | yes | **Taken.** The first time the button is pressed on a run that has seams, they are also copied to `seams_hand.json`, which nothing ever rotates away. It is in the file list. |
| Piece gap floor (Q7) | needs the shop's router bit | **Not decided for you.** The floor was raised from 0 to 1 mm only to stop a number the nester cannot honour; the real floor still needs the router bit diameter. |

## What you will see differently

### 1. Holes and arcs survive the cut (B1, B2, B3 — the biggest one)

- The 207 mm round cut-out and every filleted cut-out now survive a seam that
  cuts their panel. Before 5.4, a two-vertex circle rebuilt from the split was
  dropped, and panel-1 pieces came off the router solid. Measured on the
  reference run: panel 1's pieces now report all three cut-outs (P1-1 carries
  3 holes; before, the two pieces between them reported 2).
- Console walls and every curve that came from a cut-out's edge keep their
  arcs. The ring rebuild used to flatten every edge that did not belong to the
  ring's "dominant" loop into 1 mm chords — VCarve saw 900–2500 one-millimetre
  nodes (up to a third of a piece's perimeter). It now rebuilds each stretch
  against the loop it actually came from, and the junction chord where the kerf
  meets the boundary no longer starts an arc run one sample late.
- The plan result carries `arc_fallbacks` — how many arc runs were refitted as
  polylines because the fit failed its tolerance. Zero on a healthy split; the
  old `max_arc_rebuild_error_mm` only counted arcs that PASSED, so flattening
  was invisible.
- Piece ids now come from the list of pieces that were KEPT, so "P1-3 needs a
  seam" names the same piece after every edit (B4).
- `final_auto.dxf` / `final.dxf` reading honours the closed flag, accepts only
  CAM layers (`CAM_USER__PANEL_n`), and says so when it skips something.

### 2. The line you click is the line that is cut (A1, C4)

- A seam placed with the direction tool cuts its own chord. The audit's case:
  hover a 450 mm join beside the console, click, and the whole deck split in
  half along that line — that no longer happens. (Re-run of the audit's own
  probe: cases A and B now give one piece, control case C still two.)
- The optimiser's trial cuts are anchored at the panel's bbox centre, so a
  far-nested panel that needs a seam actually gets it instead of a seam
  floating beside it (C4).
- A seam bound to a panel whose chord misses the material is reported as
  cutting nothing, instead of silently doing nothing or nicking a corner.

### 3. The hover preview is the cut, to the last bit (A3, A5)

- The settle that runs while you hover is now the SAME correction the click
  gets — it goes through the corrector against the same reference pool. The
  old preview-vs-cut disagreement (up to 0.87 mm, and a note claiming a move
  the preview had already shown) is gone: a hovered seam needs no correction
  at all when placed (asserted at ≤ 1e-6 mm in the tests).
- Sweeping along the boat beside the console no longer blanks the preview for
  ~100 mm: after a slide, the chord kept is the one under your pointer, not
  the one nearest the slid midpoint (A3).

### 4. Seams stay on their own panel and off the edges (A4, A6, A7)

- The corrector only offers a seam the edges and seams of the panel it
  actually cuts. Cross-panel grabs (an across join jumping 24 mm onto a 40 mm
  stub on another panel, which the nest had moved next door) are gone (A6).
- Landing on a fitted edge keeps the kerf out of the material (see the table
  above). The preview no longer jumps onto the part's own edge and shaves
  3 mm off it, and no hatch gets widened (A4).
- A seam can now CONTINUE another across the console regardless of the
  300 mm reach, because end-to-end collinear seams are one join, not two (A7).

### 5. Slivers are dropped, with a warning that names the seam (A2, G26)

- A cut piece narrower than 15 mm (its true minimum width, measured on the
  smallest envelope it fits in at any turn) is dropped with a warning naming
  the seams responsible. The 1156 × 5.6 mm "toothpick" that used to be listed
  as too big, and the 5–10 mm half-metre shavings that used to be nested and
  exported, are gone.
- On the reference run's own hand seams this drops the old P1-8/P1-9 (a 385
  and a 35 mm2 sliver): the plan that read "13 pieces, 5 too big" now reads
  "11 pieces, 4 too big" — and the pieces that remain are all real.

### 6. The optimiser is honest and gentler (C1–C6)

- "On fitted edges" now means ON them: credit is only given within 1 mm of
  the edge (it used to assume the corrector would slide the cut the remaining
  25 mm — it measurably never did, so seams sat 4–9 mm off the console wall
  while the report called them aligned).
- Optimiser seams carry `snap=false`, so nothing re-snaps them at confirm or
  export and moves them off the chosen position (measured at 6.91 mm once).
- No chosen cut slices a SMALL cut-out: positions whose line reaches past a
  small cut-out's rim (8 mm in) or grazes a round one within 30 mm are refused
  (C2's 2.5–22 mm notches and the 17 mm crescent). A large structural opening
  — the console, anything over 400 mm — is deliberately exempt: seams run into
  its walls and continue, which is how a deck is actually joined around a
  console, and refusing to cross it would leave the reference boat uncuttable
  by full-span cuts at all. That exemption was measured both ways before it
  was chosen: without it the optimiser's best answer became "cannot be cut,
  two pieces still too big"; with it the cuttable layouts come back.
- A direction whose every arrangement would slice a cut-out used to zero out
  the panel's whole candidate list and drop it onto the unfiltered even-band
  fallback (which sliced) — it now offers the other direction's arrangements
  with no cuts in the empty one, and the envelope test judges the result.
- Piece count is ranked (after seam count) and stated in the headline —
  "best found: 4 sheet(s), 39.1% waste, 7 seam(s), 17 piece(s)" — so four
  extra fiddly parts can no longer be bought by a twentieth of a point of
  waste in silence (C3).
- The candidate stage gets its own slice of the time budget, and an
  out-of-time search under 50 arrangements will not replace your seams: a
  five-second budget writes nothing (C5).
- The exact-confirmation loop now budgets by the WORST confirmation it has
  seen, not the cheapest, so a short-budget press cannot run two and a half
  times over its clock.

### 7. Export and files (D1, D3, D4, G40, G41)

- A re-export that needs fewer sheets DELETES the older higher-numbered
  `sheet_NN.dxf` files, so "sheet 4 (0 pieces)" with a working download link
  cannot sit beside a two-sheet job. Files left over from an older seam layout
  are labelled "older export, the seams have changed since" until you export
  again.
- Refused pieces are marked "(not written)" in the report and on the page,
  named under the fit line, and no longer counted in a sheet's utilisation.
  Plan warnings (dropped slivers, seams that cut nothing) reach the page now,
  not just the log.
- A piece exactly 990.6 × 2006.6 mm on the 3.68° axis nests instead of being
  refused by 1.4e-13 mm (G40); oversize sizes are measured once and printed
  at one decimal, so the same piece cannot read 616 mm one line and 617 the
  next (G41).
- Settings reject a zero or non-finite sample step, nest step or any other
  numeric sheet setting instead of hanging or dividing by zero (G24).

### 8. The page and the server (E1–E8, and the low items)

- Seam ids are unique for real: a seam placed after a removal gets the next
  free name (never a duplicate), and the server rejects duplicate ids,
  zero-length seams, non-boolean snap, missing angles on angle seams and
  panels that are not whole numbers, each with a 400 naming the field.
- The seams POST carries the run id, and the page's reply-stamp checks it, so
  a request queued before you switched runs can never write one boat's seams
  over another's (E5).
- The optimiser takes its backup content at the MOMENT OF THE PRESS (in
  memory), commits it only if it is about to write, and refuses to write at
  all if seams.json changed underneath the search (E2). A refusal or a
  no-improvement press now touches nothing on disk — no more phantom
  `seams_previous.json` churn.
- Your first hand-placed set is kept forever as `seams_hand.json`, listed and
  downloadable, along with every numbered `seams_previous_N.json` (E7).
- A seam edit is saved to the run BEFORE the re-plan runs, so removing seams
  works even when the plan refuses (bad settings, no fitted geometry) — the
  one design rule the app is not allowed to break (G14/E6).
- A torn or BOM-prefixed seams.json reads as "no seams" with a `.bak` kept,
  instead of bricking the run; writes go through a temp file and fall back to
  the in-place write only when Windows refuses the rename (E8).
- Editing waits for the run's own seams to load before posting, so a click in
  the first half-second cannot overwrite a hand layout with one seam (E3).
  Export and the optimiser wait for unsaved edits (E4). Tab switches no
  longer nest twice (F31). Job polling stops after a server restart instead
  of logging forever (F29). "Removed 4 seams" says whether it saved (F30).
  Errors read as sentences — "best seam layout failed", not
  "optimise_seams failed" or "ValueError: …" (F23).
- The flat view turns bow-up correctly even before auto-fit has run (F48);
  the dead `/api/pick` route is gone (F33); `AutoDeck.lnk` now takes its icon
  from `D:\AutoDeck\AutoDeck.ico` instead of the old C: copy (F50).
- Version is 5.4.0 everywhere (page title, brand, `/api/state`, ui_smoke).

## What was measured, and what was not

Measured (on staged copies of the reference run, through the real
`sheetjob.plan`):

- the reference run's hand-seam plan: 11 pieces (was 13), 4 oversize (was 5),
  P1-1 carrying 3 holes (was 2 across both pieces), arc fallbacks 0;
- the audit's A1 probe: cases A and B → 1 piece, control C → 2;
- the nester old-vs-new on both fixtures' new pieces: bit-identical layout,
  summary, warnings and used areas;
- the browser pass, `tools/ui_smoke.py`: 84/84 checks (tabs, seam tool,
  remove/clear/undo, a failing save, the cut-out drawn as a cut-out, the
  optimiser report wording) — this script had never been run before;
- the engine-v1 boundary suite: 116 passed, unchanged.

Not measured: VCarve's behaviour on the new files, and a physical cut.
Manufacturing approval remains TEST_ONLY until a cut has been verified.

## The frozen nesting table

Steps 2/3/4 changed the PIECES the nester receives (the corrector no longer
slides the hand seams onto other panels' geometry, so they settle a few
millimetres from where they used to, and two of the slivers they shaved off
panel 1 now fall under the area floor). By the re-capture procedure
(`HANDOFF_FOR_NEXT_AI.md` §7.6) the table in `test_nesting_speed.py` was
re-captured from the COMMITTED nester at `958d8f0` (extracted via `git show`,
import patched), after proving the current nester is bit-identical to it on
the new pieces, both fixtures, every comparison `==`. The diff:

- AXIS run: the P1-9 row is gone (that sliver no longer exists); P4 and P5
  gained 10 mm of y (the space P1-9 left on sheet 0); placed count 8 → 7;
  unplaced loses P1-8; utilisation sheet 0: 0.5888 → 0.5905.
- NO-AXIS run: every row unchanged except P1-17's origin, which moved by
  4e-11 mm of float noise from the ring rebuild. Summary unchanged.

## What was deliberately NOT changed

- The nester's search itself (audit step 10, D2 multi-order, D3 touching
  rule, G36 buffer resolution) — that needs your call on the router bit and
  the "try other orders" option, and the frozen rule says the default path
  must not move.
- The 1 mm faceting question (Q13) is closed as far as code can take it —
  the faceting is gone — but whether VCarve cared still needs a cut.
- macOS launchers, distribution, the USB-stick-vs-internal-drive question.
